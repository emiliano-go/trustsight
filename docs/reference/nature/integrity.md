## Where it comes from

A `sha256sums` line is the recipe's **tamper-evident seal**: it turns the
tarball into a parcel sealed with wax, and the promise is "what you open will
be what I sealed". The integrity family is about breaking that promise in a
way that still looks intact - the seal was lifted, the contents swapped, and
the wax pressed back on.

The rules cover the ways the seal is defeated: `SKIP` and placeholder sums,
sums generated by the build itself, `md5sums`/`sha1sums` where a stronger
algorithm belongs, and the source URL mutating after verification. Each one
means the same thing: the thing that gets built is not vouched for by the
recipe at all.

This is why the checksum rules sit close to the fetch rules in importance: an
unverified source makes fetch-and-execute far more dangerous, because the
reviewer can no longer reason about what was fetched.

## What a finding means

- The finding says the verification is defeated or absent, and where. A
  `SKIP` or a freshly-computed sum means the tarball's contents are not
  vouched for by the recipe at all.
- Integrity findings are strong amplifiers: a weakened checksum beside a
  dynamic source is a much higher risk than either alone.
- Weak-algorithm findings are about hygiene meeting a real bar; read them as
  "this verification will not stop a substitution".
